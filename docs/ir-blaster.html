The Optical IR Blaster

The Flipper's own infrared LED is weak and points one way. This booster listens to that beam with a photodiode, cleans it up, and fires it back out at high power in every direction — with no GPIO tap, no firmware change, and not a single wire running to the Flipper. The only input is light.

Three things that make or break it

Purely optical input

No signal or power wire runs to the Flipper. Its own IR LED lights the BPW34 and the booster is galvanically isolated — nothing to flash, nothing to solder into the Flipper. Aim the Flipper's IR window at the photodiode and that's the whole interface.

Shield the feedback path

The ring emits the same 940 nm the BPW34 is straining to hear. With no baffle between them the booster latches on and self-oscillates. Mount an opaque shroud so the photodiode only ever sees the Flipper — this is the ✕ on the schematic.

Calibrate the threshold

Turn RV1 until the LM393 trips cleanly on the Flipper's beam but stays quiet on room light and sunlight. Too sensitive and daylight keys the ring; too dull and weak commands are dropped. It's the one thing you tune after assembly.
